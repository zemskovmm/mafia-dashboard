# IDD-0001.spec-club-access-and-player-identity

## Intent

Provide a club-isolated, host-operated dashboard for in-person Mafia games while respecting player consent and preserving shared game history.

## Related Specifications

- IDD-0002 defines participants, roles and game rules.
- IDD-0004 defines game-record access, correction and deletion effects.
- IDD-0005 defines preservation when stored data is incompatible.

## Behavior

### Accounts and clubs

- Users authenticate through Google sign-in. An authenticated user may create a club without a fee; club creation requires the creator's personal-data consent and creates a linked manager profile.
- The server authorizes access against club membership. Selecting or supplying a club identifier does not grant access to that club's data.
- Managers and regular club users may host games and list, open, create, save and delete game records in their own club. Club and player-profile administration remain manager privileges.
- Withdrawing consent for the creator's player profile does not silently remove the creator's club-management access.
- Game records and secret host data are not anonymously accessible. An authenticated, redacted event view is not a public spectator service.

### Player identity and participation

- A player has a required nickname, unique within the club without regard to case.
- Without personal-data consent, the player profile retains only the nickname as personal identity data; non-identifying built-in role-icon preferences are also permitted. Storing personal identity details or uploaded role images requires consent and requires first name, last name and email.
- Each player has an opaque, permanent identity scoped to the club. Names and consented personal details belong to the erasable player profile, not the identity carried by game records. A retired identity is never assigned to a different player or offered for new human participation.
- A game host is a separate saved human profile and cannot simultaneously occupy a player seat in that game. Virtual Casper participation is governed by IDD-0002, not by a bypass of account or club authorization.

### Personal role icons

- Managers may independently choose Citizen, Mafia, Don and Sheriff artwork for each club player, using a searchable local English/Russian icon library or a static PNG, JPEG or WebP upload. Choices persist in the player's profile across games and authorized devices. There is no player self-service or live cross-device synchronization requirement.
- Each role can return to its default independently. Editing and cancelling do not publish changes; failed saves retain the prior profile. Updates omitting icon preferences or individual roles preserve those choices, subject to consent and deletion rules. Existing profiles without choices use bundled defaults.
- Uploads require current consent, bounded bytes and decoded pixels, actual supported-image validation and metadata-free thumbnail normalization. Reject unsupported, animated, corrupt, oversized or falsely labelled images rather than trusting extensions or MIME declarations. Do not import remote image URLs or retain original filenames/metadata.
- Uploaded images remain private and club/player-owned. Authorized members can read that player's complete bounded media bundle, independent of assigned game role; manager-only profile mutation and linked-owner protections apply. Foreign references never grant access. Profile lists carry lightweight selectors rather than all media bytes.
- Consent withdrawal detaches uploaded selections and media, using defaults for affected roles while retaining built-in choices. Retirement erases all icon preferences and media. Replacement, reset and individual deletion revoke obsolete media authority atomically; physical cleanup is recoverable and must not be falsely described as complete while pending. Whole-club deletion revokes access to these assets under the separate retention contract below; existing exported backup retention is unchanged.
- Appearance never enters game snapshots, canonical events, results or replay hashes. Hidden roles use the neutral person, not a role-specific custom image. Missing or unavailable images fall back safely; stale loads cannot cross club, player or profile versions.

### Deletion and retention

- A player participating in an active game, whether as host or participant, cannot be deleted.
- When deletion is permitted, retire that player's identity and erase the profile nickname, consented personal details and profile-derived user links/access. Retain the opaque identity, completed shared games, anonymous individual participation/results and other players' results. Profile administration and new-game selection exclude retired identities; historical displays resolve current profiles separately and show a localized anonymous fallback for retired or unavailable profiles.
- Identifying profile changes or deletion do not change the meaning or integrity of supported identity-only game histories. Historical preservation after deletion applies to supported identity-only records; incompatible older named records are handled under IDD-0005, not silently anonymized.
- Club deletion immediately makes the club unavailable and prevents stale saves from restoring its application data. Existing record/file purge attempts and authorized manual recovery remain, but neither immediate nor eventual erasure of every private stored file is guaranteed. Files from outstanding uploads may remain indefinitely without later intervention.
- Club-deletion confirmation and successful results distinguish revoked application access from unguaranteed physical erasure. They do not promise scheduled cleanup, automatic retries or a future erasure deadline. Failed deletion requests retain their error behavior rather than reporting success.
- Clearing browser data does not delete server-side clubs, players, game records or results.

## Product-Significant Architecture And Constraints

Club isolation and consent enforcement are server-side security boundaries. Client selection, a guessed record identifier or a permitted action in another club must not bypass them. Retained identity references preserve individual game meaning without keeping erased profile fields in supported game material. Free-text moderator reasons remain free text, not an automatically redacted identity field.

## Non-Goals

- Anonymous game-record access, a public spectator service or a player-phone game client.
- Granting regular members club or player-profile administration merely because they may host games.
- Prescribing a database schema or a particular anonymized placeholder representation.

## Acceptance Criteria

1. A regular member can manage game records in their club but cannot manage another club's records or perform manager-only administration.
2. Missing authentication, forged club selection and cross-club record identifiers do not expose or mutate protected data.
3. Nickname-only participation, consented personal details and consent withdrawal respect their respective data boundaries.
4. Setup rejects a host who also occupies a player seat.
5. Active hosting or participation blocks player deletion. Permitted deletion erases the profile, retains the same opaque identity and completed outcomes, including anonymous individual and other-player results, without keeping copied names in supported game records.
6. Browser clearing and server-side deletion have distinct, observable effects.
7. Retired identities cannot be selected for new games or reassigned; profile changes and deletion do not alter supported canonical replay integrity. Whole-club deletion removes the club's identities.
8. All four independent icon choices survive save, reload and a fresh authorized device. Search, upload, reset, Cancel and failed Save respect draft boundaries; omitted preferences preserve prior choices. Built-ins work without consent; upload validation, consent withdrawal, retirement and club deletion enforce the private-media lifecycle without changing game history.
9. Whole-club deletion reports application deletion separately from unguaranteed physical erasure, including when an upload finishes late. The former club cannot be accessed or restored by that upload; another club remains unaffected. Confirmation and the result notice explain the retention limitation in both supported languages without implying background cleanup.

## Verification

Exercise actual profile controls in both languages and fresh authorized contexts. Read back HTTP results and private storage for valid and invalid uploads, retained references, manager/member/anonymous/foreign-club requests and linked-owner edits. Verify normalized dimensions and stripped metadata, atomic failure/retry, replacement/reset/withdrawal/retirement cleanup, live-asset garbage-collection retention and backup/restore. Inspect masked cards and departure faces for role-specific DOM, accessibility or request leaks; late responses and club/version changes must not restore stale images. Compare exact canonical material before and after appearance changes.

Exercise the permission matrix with managers, regular members, unauthenticated actors and members of a different club. Verify server responses and persisted effects, not only hidden UI controls. Cover consent changes, active hosting/participation and deletion using completed games shared with other players. Verify stable opaque identities and replay hashes, retained individual and other-player results, erased profile fields, localized anonymous display, and exclusion from new selection. Check both individual and whole-club deletion without assuming old named records have been anonymized.

Overlap whole-club deletion with an outstanding upload through independent storage clients. Verify the explicit deletion/physical-erasure distinction, retained access denial and stale-publication rejection, and preservation of another club. Do not require physical erasure as this scenario's success criterion. Exercise the actual deletion confirmation/result in English and Russian, cancellation, storage failure and original-manager-only manual recovery; retain ordinary purge coverage separately.
